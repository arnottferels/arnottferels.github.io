Good morning in different languages around the world

Jul 2024

Image: Renata Daniel
greetingsculturelanguage
Cite
BibTeX
Share

“Good morning” is a universal greeting, but it takes different forms across the world.

In Asia, people greet in various ways. In China, Taiwan, and Singapore, it’s 早上好 (Zǎo shàng hǎo).1,2,3 Japan uses おはようございます (Ohayō gozaimasu),4 while in South Korea, it’s 좋은 아침입니다 (Joheun achimimnida).5 In India, Hindi and Bengali speakers say सुप्रभात (Suprabhat),6,7 and in Thailand, it’s สวัสดีตอนเช้า (Sawasdee ton chao).8 Vietnam greets with Chào buổi sáng,9 and in Iran, it’s صبح بخیر (Sobh bekheir).10 Arabic speakers in countries like Saudi Arabia, Egypt, and the UAE use صباح الخير (Sabah al-khair),11,12,13 and in Israel, it’s בוקר טוב (Boker tov).14

In Europe, English speakers in the UK, Ireland, and Malta say “Good Morning”.15,16,17 In Spain, it’s “Buenos Días”,18 and in France, Belgium, and Switzerland, people greet with “Bonjour”.19,20,21 Germany, Austria, and Switzerland use “Guten Morgen”,22,23,21 while Italy says “Buongiorno”.24 In Portugal and Brazil, it’s “Bom Dia”,25,26 and in Russia, people say Доброе утро (Dobroye utro).27 Dutch speakers in Netherlands and Belgium greet with “Goedemorgen”,2820 and in Greece, it’s Καλημέρα (Kaliméra).29 In Scandinavia, Sweden, Norway, and Denmark use “God morgon” or “God morgen”, and in Finland, it’s “Hyvää huomenta”.30,31,32,33

In North America, USA and Canada use “Good morning”,34,35 while Spanish speakers in Mexico, Central America, and the Caribbean greet with “Buenos Días”.36 In South America, Argentina, Colombia, and Chile also say “Buenos Días”,37,38,39 while Brazilians use “Bom Dia”.26 In Oceania, both Australia and New Zealand say “Good Morning”.40,41
